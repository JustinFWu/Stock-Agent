Stock-Agent · Build Roadmap

Vol-targeted momentum, and the system that trades it.

The alpha search returned a clean negative. This is the pivot: stop predicting returns, start predicting volatility — and put a real autonomous system underneath a strategy that is documented rather than discovered.

unscaled — risk drifts with the market vol-targeted — risk held constant by design

Thesis

One problem is unwinnable. The adjacent one is not.

Forward returns on the 71 US mega-caps this repo tested are, as far as it can measure, unpredictable from price history. That was tested properly and it failed properly. Volatility is a different animal: realized vol is strongly autocorrelated, so a forecast of next week's vol is a forecast that can actually be made — and it has direct economic value in deciding position size.

The direction comes from somewhere honest instead. 12–2 momentum is the most replicated anomaly in the literature, holding across centuries, countries, and asset classes. Its defining flaw is the momentum crash — the long/short losing 30–50% in weeks coming off a market bottom. The published fix is scaling exposure by a forecast of the strategy's own volatility, which roughly doubles the realized Sharpe.

So the two halves are not bolted together. The vol forecast is the documented remedy for momentum's documented weakness. That is the thesis, and everything below is in service of testing it honestly and then trading it unattended.

Architecture

Four layers, and one of them must be dumb.

Signal

12–2 momentum over the universe. Answers what and which direction. Long-only, top decile, for v1.

Sizing

Vol forecast → shrunk covariance → target weights at a fixed portfolio vol. Answers how much. Continuous, model-driven, allowed to be wrong.

Risk · veto

Gross and net caps, per-name and per-sector limits, drawdown kill switch, data-staleness breaker. Hard, auditable, and non-negotiable.

Execution

Broker-agnostic interface. Reconcile actual positions against targets, submit the diff, log every fill. Idempotent across a mid-rebalance crash.

Monitoring

Deterministic checks fire the alerts and the kill switch. An LLM may triage, summarize, and explain — never decide.

Design test for the risk layer: if a limit ever becomes a penalty term inside the optimizer, it has stopped being a risk control and become a suggestion.

Sequence

Ordered by dependency, not by appetite.

Each phase is numbered because each one genuinely blocks the next — every number computed out of order is a number that can't be trusted. Gates are pre-committed here, before any result is known, for the same reason the last no-go was trustworthy.

Cleared

Split- and dividend-adjusted daily OHLCV for the 82-name universe, cached as parquet alongside the existing feature store.

2infrastructure

Backtester with real costs

Cleared
Deliverable
An event-driven backtest carrying commissions, spread, and a market-impact estimate. No-trade bands so that continuously resizing to hit an exact vol target doesn't churn away the benefit.
Why here
Nothing downstream can be evaluated without it, and most retail edges die entirely on costs. Building it before the strategy keeps the strategy honest.
As built
Decide after the close of day t, fill at the open of t+1 — a signal can never be traded at a price used to compute it. src/backtest/ holds the engine, the cost model, portfolio accounting and metrics; PricePanel.as_of is the one look-ahead firewall.
Gate — met One function produces both the backtest weights and the live weights, enforced by a test asserting byte-identical output for a fixed date. target_weights() is that function; test_weight_parity.py compares SHA-256 digests of the serialised weights, with no tolerance. The risk limits live in config.py, so neither caller holds its own copy.
3strategy

Signal, sizing, and the risk layer

Deliverable
12–2 momentum (formation t−12 to t−2, skipping the most recent month) → top-decile long-only selection → vol-targeted weights from a Ledoit–Wolf shrunk covariance → the hard risk layer.
Why shrinkage
Correlations spike in exactly the drawdowns you're sizing against. Naive inverse-vol weighting understates portfolio risk when it matters most; shrinkage is one scikit-learn call and it is the difference between a vol target that holds and one that blows through.
Before code
Three settings already carry notes in the source and are wrong at their current values. MIN_HISTORY_DAYS rises 252 → ~273: a 12–2 formation window reaches back thirteen months, so a name with exactly 252 bars would rank on a truncated, higher-vol window against names measured over a full one. cash_annual_rate defaults to 0 and must be set deliberately — vol targeting holds a lot of cash, and crediting it nothing understates the strategy. And vol-targeted weights are proposal_scale = "absolute", the case that machinery was built for: a sum of 0.7 is a deliberate 30% cash position, which is exactly what a naive normaliser would silently spend.
Gate — rewritten 2026-09-12, before any Phase 3 number existed The original — net-of-cost Sharpe above ~0.4 — was unfalsifiable on this universe and has been replaced. The reasoning is below, under The gate that could not fail.
  1. Primary Information ratio of the strategy against EqualWeightStrategy, both net of costs on the identical panel, engine and cost model: > 0.2. Differencing removes what the two portfolios share. This is the gate.
  2. Secondary Vol targeting must measurably improve risk-adjusted return over unscaled momentum. Unchanged, and the actual test of the thesis: if scaling adds nothing, the sizing layer is decoration.
  3. Floor Net Sharpe at or above the measured equal-weight baseline — about 0.87 here, not 0.4. Necessary, never sufficient: a strategy that cannot beat doing nothing on its own universe has not earned an information ratio.
And binding A passing Phase 3 is provisional until re-run on point-in-time data. None of the three tests above removes survivorship — they only narrow what it can explain. The Phase 4 paper record is the first genuinely out-of-sample evidence this project will ever have.

Phase 1 result

The forecast beats the baselines, by a modest and steady margin.

Five expanding walk-forward folds with a five-day embargo, over 430,185 rows across 82 names and 5,344 trading days from 2005 to 2026. Every model scores the identical out-of-sample rows. Lower is better on both metrics.

HAR-RV3, refit each fold 0.57570.1506 0 / 51 / 5

The model wins QLIKE on every fold and RMSE on four of five, losing fold one — the 2008–2012 block — to HAR by 0.0012, which is noise. Nothing here depends on a single regime, which is the specific failure the gate was written to catch.

Margins of 5–9% are worth having and are not a discovery. The honest reading: volatility is genuinely forecastable, most of that forecastability is already in EWMA and HAR, and the model adds a real but incremental slice on top. That is the expected shape of a true result in a well-studied problem.

The bug that first read as a failed gate

The first run failed. XGBoost swept RMSE but lost QLIKE to EWMA on all five folds — an odd pattern, since a model that forecasts better by one measure rarely loses that consistently by another.

The cause was retransformation bias. Fitting log(vol) under squared-error loss and exponentiating yields the conditional median, and volatility is right-skewed, so the median sits below the mean. Measured directly: forecasts ran 9.1% low. QLIKE is deliberately asymmetric against under-forecasting, so it punished that hard, while EWMA — which happened to over-forecast by 10% — was rewarded for the same reason.

The fix is Duan's smearing estimator, mean(exp(residual)) computed on training residuals only, applied to both log-target models. It measured 1.099 against an observed bias of 1/0.9085 = 1.101 — effectively exact. Correcting it also lifted HAR substantially (QLIKE 0.690 to 0.576), making it a much stronger baseline than the first run suggested. XGBoost still clears it.

Worth recording because the correction is now load-bearing in production: it is saved inside the model artifact and applied by predict_vol. Loading the estimator and calling it directly would leave every forecast about 9% low — and since sizing is inverse to forecast volatility, an under-forecast reads as a calmer market and takes roughly 10% more exposure per name than intended, before the per-name cap binds. The forecast is biased low; the position is biased large.

Six bugs that were latent on an equal-weight book and live on Phase 3's

Every one of these was found by review rather than by a failing number, because on a never-trading equal-weight placeholder none of them moved the output much. Each is the kind that produces a plausible curve rather than a crash, and each would have been load-bearing the moment a vol-targeted strategy started resizing daily.

Buy scaling measured buying power against raw notional and ignored the costs that are the shortfall — 525 days of unpriced margin, and a breached gross ceiling under stress. A minimum commission does not scale with order size, so the naive fix still went cash-negative; affordability is now solved by bisection. A missing open bar valued a holding at zero, collapsing NAV and selling down every healthy position — and the first fix for that read today's close, which is not knowable at today's open: measured, one missing open turned another name's order into a 125-share sale. Constraints clipped before scaling, collapsing any conviction-scored strategy to equal weight with its signal silently deleted. Turnover and cost drag divided by mean NAV, understating both by ~18% on a compounding curve. And derived feature caches were trusted whenever the raw manifest still named the same window, so a --refetch reused features built from bars that no longer existed.

All six are fixed, each with a regression test; the suite stands at 127. The reason to record them here is the pattern, not the fixes: a backtester validated only against a strategy that barely trades is not validated.

The binding constraint

Survivorship, and the gate it quietly broke.

The gate that could not fail

The Phase 3 gate as originally written — net-of-cost Sharpe above ~0.4 across the full history — is passable by something with no edge at all. Equal-weighting all 82 names and never trading clears it at 0.87. Even honest point-in-time RSP clears it at 0.58. A gate that cannot fail is not a gate.

There is a second, independent defect. The 0.3–0.5 figure from the momentum literature is a long/short number, whose benchmark is zero. It was attached to a long-only strategy, whose benchmark is the universe. Those are not comparable quantities, and no threshold picked for one means anything applied to the other.

This is a deal-breaker for the gate, not for the project — and the honest moment to say so is now, before a Phase 3 number exists. The replacement is in the Phase 3 card above.

The measurement, for scale: equal-weight daily-rebalanced over this universe returns 16.8%/yr at Sharpe 0.91 and −47% max drawdown, against 10.1%/yr, Sharpe 0.58 and −60% for RSP, a point-in-time equal-weight S&P 500. Roughly 6.7pp/yr and 0.33 of Sharpe. That is an indication of scale and not an isolated measurement of survivorship — the two portfolios differ in composition and rebalancing as well as in hindsight. It is why every --backtest run computes a same-universe baseline by construction rather than by discipline.

None of this needs an architecture change, which was the point of building the seam early. UniverseSpec.members_asof() returns everything on every date today and is a no-op waiting for a real membership table; PricePanel.tradable_as_of answers the separate question of whether a name had a bar, off the price data. Drop a point-in-time table into the first and nothing in the engine, the strategy or the weight path is touched.

What will need attention is the engine assumption that pairs with it: a name whose bars stop is currently marked forward at its last close indefinitely and can never be sold. Harmless on survivors. A real problem the day delisted names arrive, and it is written down in engine.py for that reason.

yfinance cannot supply any of it. It has no delisted coverage and silently serves recycled symbols — FB returns an ETF, BBBY returns Overstock's path — so splicing it in trades a bounded, documented error for an unbounded one that looks like signal.

Decisions

Calls made, with the reasoning attached.

12–2 and 12–1 are the same thing

The standard factor forms on returns from t−12 to t−2. Some call it 12–1 for the skipped month, some 12–2 for where the window ends. Whatever it's called, the skip is load-bearing — it dodges short-term reversal.

Long-only for v1

Most of momentum's crash risk lives in the short leg, and going long-only sheds borrow, locates, and squeezes at the same time. Shorting is a later decision, made with live data rather than backtested optimism.

Alpaca before IBKR

IBKR is the better long-run venue, but it wants a running gateway, a socket API, and a daily re-auth cycle. Gateway plumbing should not block the first working loop. The interface makes the switch cheap later.

No LLM in the money path

Deterministic checks own the alerts and the kill switch. The model may triage an anomaly, summarize the overnight session, and draft the daily note. It never sizes, sends, or cancels. That boundary lives in the code, not in intent.

Paper trading is the only real evidence

Every backtest is self-graded, and this one is survivorship-biased on top. The forward paper record is the first genuinely out-of-sample sample this project will ever have.

Expectations, set honestly

Momentum has decayed in US mega-caps. Raw long/short Sharpe of roughly 0.3–0.5, better after scaling. Real, useful, and not spectacular — which is the point.

Carried forward

The result this plan is built on top of.

Cross-sectional ranking — tested, no-go (2026-07-27)

A pooled XGBoost ranker on cross-sectionally demeaned 5-day forward returns, over 71 names and roughly two years of daily bars. The pre-committed gate was mean rank IC above 0.03, stable across folds, with a long/short Sharpe above 1.

Folds disagreed in sign and cancelled. No individual feature cleared the gate; the best, atr_pct, reached IC 0.0255 at an information ratio of 0.09. A synthetic control with a planted signal recovered IC ~0.49, confirming the harness detects signal when signal exists.

Two conclusions carry into this branch. First, the harness and the pre-commitment discipline are sound and are being reused. Second, the information set was the problem: standard indicators on the most heavily arbitraged names in the world. This roadmap responds by changing the question rather than retuning the answer.
